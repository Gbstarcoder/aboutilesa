History

Ilesa is a historic Yoruba town in Osun State, southwestern Nigeria. It is the traditional headquarters of the Ijesa people and has played an important role in Yoruba history. According to Ijesa tradition, the town was founded by Owa Obokun Ajibogun, a son of Oduduwa, the legendary ancestor of the Yoruba people. Tradition states that Ajibogun and his followers migrated from Ile-Ife and eventually settled in the area that became Ilesa. The town later developed into the centre of the Ijesa kingdom, ruled by the Owa of Ijesaland. Ilesa became an important trading and political centre because of its location and the activities of its people. The Ijesa were also well known for farming, craftsmanship and long-distance trade. During the colonial period, Ilesa came under British administration. After Nigeria gained independence in 1960, the town continued to grow as an important commercial and cultural centre. Today, Ilesa remains an important city in Osun State and the cultural heart of Ijesaland. Its history, traditional institutions and festivals continue to preserve the heritage of the Ijesa people.

The King

The current king of Ilesa and the paramount ruler of Ijesaland is His Imperial Majesty, Owa Obokun Adimula Oba Clement Adesuyi Haastrup, Ajimoko III. He succeeded the late Oba Gabriel Adekunle Aromolaran II, who reigned from 1982 until his death in September 2024. Oba Clement Adesuyi Haastrup was crowned Owa Obokun Adimula of Ijesaland on 23 May 2025 in Ilesa.
